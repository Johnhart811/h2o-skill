# Voice, Channel Strategy, Content Traceability & Standard Output

Read this for Stage K (before writing) and Stage M (audit) of the main
workflow.

---

## Voice and Style

The content should sound like a highly knowledgeable person explaining
something important to another human — not a brand talking at a customer.
The reader's reaction should be "someone actually understands my situation,"
never "someone is trying to sell me something."

**Use:** conversational language, specificity, confidence, empathy,
directness, natural rhythm, clear explanations, varied sentence length.

**Avoid entirely:** generic hype, corporate jargon, robotic language,
meaningless superlatives, fake enthusiasm, repetitive AI-sounding patterns,
empty claims, unnecessary persuasion language, and default phrases like
"revolutionary," "game-changing," "unlock your potential," "take your
business to the next level," "industry-leading," "world-class" — unless
there's a specific, factual reason the word is actually accurate here.

If a sentence would sound out of place said face-to-face to someone you
respect, cut it.

## Channel Strategy

Don't copy one message across every channel unchanged. For each relevant
channel, determine: where the audience is active, where they research,
where they compare, where they ask questions, where they seek proof, and
where they actually make decisions. Then for each channel, set: **where,
when, how often, format, message, buyer-journey stage, and objective.** The
same underlying strategy from earlier stages needs different execution per
channel.

### Advertising Intelligence (when researching visible competitor ads)
Investigate hooks, headlines, offers, creative style, promises, proof, CTAs,
positioning, audience targeting clues, landing-page structure, campaign
themes, timing, frequency, and channels. Distinguish three separate things:
**what they're doing**, **why they may be doing it**, and **whether it
appears to actually work** — the third requires real evidence (engagement,
longevity of the campaign, reviews), not an assumption that visibility
equals effectiveness.

## Content Traceability — the pre-write spec

Before writing any single asset, specify:

- Target audience / segment
- Buyer-journey awareness stage
- Dominant desire (and the conflicting desire it needs to resolve)
- The problem being addressed
- The specific belief being changed, and which belief-ladder rung(s)
- The objection(s) being pre-answered
- The decision criterion (or criteria) being established
- The presale phase(s) it owns
- The certainty level(s) it's responsible for
- Proof required (and whether it currently exists, or is a PROOF GAP)
- CTA / next step (or explicitly: none — this piece's job is to advance a
  phase, not close)

After writing, audit each major section: if a sentence doesn't increase
relevance, clarify the problem, strengthen a useful belief, remove
uncertainty, establish differentiation, provide proof, advance the decision,
or make the next step clearer — question whether it belongs.

## Decision Simplification

When an audience faces too many options (including within the operator's
own offer — packages, tiers, add-ons), reduce unnecessary choices, create
clear categories, explain trade-offs plainly, and make the next step
obvious. When appropriate and genuinely warranted, it's fine for content to
say: *"Based on what you've told me, this is the option I'd recommend, and
here's why."* Don't confuse the customer with complexity that doesn't serve
them.

## Standard Output Format

When the operator asks for a full strategic analysis (not just a single
asset), structure the deliverable as:

1. Executive Diagnosis
2. Offer Diagnosis
3. Customer Profile
4. Desire Hierarchy
5. Conflicting Desires
6. Scattered Focus / Decision Concentration
7. Frustration & Failed-Attempt Map
8. Belief Map
9. Objection Map
10. Competitive & Alternative Analysis
11. Message Saturation
12. Market Gap
13. Decision Criteria
14. Unique Mechanism
15. Positioning
16. Proof / Trust / Risk Architecture
17. Belief Ladder
18. Presale Sequence
19. Content System
20. Channel Strategy
21. Presale Readiness Score
22. Failure Analysis (if applicable — live content already underperforming)
23. Content / Campaign Execution
24. Final Recommendations

For a single-asset request (one post, one ad, one email), skip the full
report — work the relevant stages from SKILL.md silently, produce the
Content Traceability spec briefly, then the asset itself. Reserve the full
24-section format for when the operator actually wants the strategy
document, not every time content is requested.

## The Presale Audit

Run this against every finished piece or campaign before presenting it. If
several answers are genuinely "no," go back to the relevant stage rather
than polishing the copy further — polishing doesn't fix a missing stage.

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | Desire — identified beyond the surface layer? |
| 2 | Conflict — the competing desire addressed, not ignored? |
| 3 | Focus — the decision simplified to one clear path? |
| 4 | Problem — the real problem established, not the assumed one? |
| 5 | Frustration — previous failures acknowledged specifically? |
| 6 | Beliefs — the actual blocking belief identified and addressed? |
| 7 | Objections — the relevant ones pre-answered? |
| 8 | Solution — explained by mechanism, not adjectives? |
| 9 | Differentiation — criteria-based, not manufactured inferiority? |
| 10 | Competition — realistic alternatives considered, including doing nothing? |
| 11 | Proof — real evidence provided, or its absence flagged? |
| 12 | Trust — is it clear why this provider specifically? |
| 13 | Risk — reduced honestly, not claimed away? |
| 14 | Decision — is the next step obvious, low-pressure, and singular? |

Report the audit honestly, including weak or unresolved items — the audit
is only useful if failures are surfaced, not smoothed over.
